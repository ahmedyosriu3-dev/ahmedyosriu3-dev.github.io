Symbol logos

Every ticker carries a logo so you can find a row by shape before you have read a word of it. Here is what sits behind that:

  • Your browser never talks to a logo provider. Pages point at this app’s own /logo/<TICKER> route. The server fetches the image once, stores it in data/logos/, and serves it from disk after that — so your watchlist is not broadcast to a third party, and a slow provider can never slow a page down twice.
  • A coloured monogram means “no artwork”, not “broken”. Bonds, commodity trusts and obscure tickers often have no logo anywhere. The tile is drawn locally, and its colour is derived from the ticker, so a symbol always looks the same — which is all you need to recognise a row.
  • Failed lookups are remembered for a week. A missing symbol costs one request, not one per page view. Offline, everything falls back to monograms and nothing blocks.

Using it well: leave it alone. It warms itself as you browse and then costs nothing. Hit Refresh logos in Settings after a rebrand, or once after you were offline when symbols were first added — that is what clears the week-long misses. Treat a logo as decoration only: it is matched on ticker alone, so never use it to confirm which company you are about to buy — read the ticker and the name. If you would rather the app made no outbound requests at all, set LOGOS_ENABLED=false in .env and every symbol uses a monogram.
