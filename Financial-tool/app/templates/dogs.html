A list that changes once a year, on purpose
On the last trading day of the year the thirty Dow components are ranked by dividend yield. The top ten are the Dogs; you hold them equally weighted for the whole of the next calendar year and ignore everything that happens in between. The five cheapest by share price are the Small Dogs — the same idea with more of it.
The theory is that a high yield on a Dow company usually means the price has fallen rather than the dividend has risen, so the screen buys large businesses when they are unloved. It is a value tilt with a good story, not a law: it trailed the index through most of the 2010s, and a yield can be high because the dividend is about to be cut.
